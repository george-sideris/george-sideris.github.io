I work on learning-based manipulation for heavy machinery. My master's research at the [Aerospace Mechatronics Lab](https://aerospacemechatronics.lab.mcgill.ca/) with [Prof. Inna Sharf](https://www.mcgill.ca/mecheng/people/staff/inna-sharf) teaches a hydraulic forestry crane to clear dense log piles: a policy that reads raw point clouds decides where to sink the grapple, is trained by imitation and reinforcement learning in a GPU-parallel Isaac Lab simulation, and is then deployed on a physical crane testbed. The work is supported by an FRQNT master's scholarship and a Mitacs Accelerate award with FPInnovations.

Before that, I led a four-student team at the [NeuroImaging and Surgical Technologies Lab](https://nist.mni.mcgill.ca/) (Prof. D. Louis Collins) building [LARK](/projects/lark/), a low-cost multi-camera optical tracker for image-guided surgery that reaches sub-millimeter accuracy for about a tenth of the price of commercial systems. I co-founded [OPSIS](/cv/) to carry that work toward the operating room.

I am interested in robot learning, sim-to-real transfer, and perception for contact-rich manipulation, and I like problems where the hardware has to work at the end.

The best feedback loop is learning to build and building to learn.
