I work on learning-based manipulation. Since October 2026 I am a Robotics Research Engineer at Bracket Bot in San Francisco, and in January 2027 I start a Ph.D. in Computer Science at McGill University and [Mila](https://mila.quebec/en) with [Prof. David Meger](https://www.cim.mcgill.ca/~dmeger/), supported by an FRQNT doctoral research scholarship. My interests are moving toward world models and reinforcement learning for manipulation.

My M.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering at McGill (expected Fall 2026), with [Prof. Inna Sharf](https://www.mcgill.ca/mecheng/people/staff/inna-sharf) at the [Aerospace Mechatronics Lab](https://aerospacemechatronics.lab.mcgill.ca/), taught a hydraulic forestry crane to clear dense log piles. A policy that reads raw point clouds decides where to sink the grapple, is trained by imitation and reinforcement learning in a GPU-parallel Isaac Lab simulation, and was deployed unchanged in field trials on the crane, where it cleared more of the pile than the heuristic previously in use ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07613), under review at ICRA 2027). Part of that work was a Mitacs Accelerate internship with FPInnovations.

Before that, I led a four-student team at the [NeuroImaging and Surgical Technologies Lab](https://nist.mni.mcgill.ca/) (Prof. D. Louis Collins) at [The Neuro](https://www.mcgill.ca/neuro/) (Montreal Neurological Institute-Hospital) building [LARK](/projects/lark/), a multi-camera optical tracker for image-guided surgery that reaches sub-millimeter accuracy with under US$1,000 of hardware ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07561), submitted to Medical Image Analysis). I co-founded OPSIS through McGill Engine's TechAccel program to carry that work toward the operating room.

The best feedback loop is learning to build and building to learn.
