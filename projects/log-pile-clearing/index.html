Learning log pile clearing

Grasp targeting from raw point clouds, trained in simulation and deployed unchanged on a hydraulic forestry crane

Mill yards receive wood in dense piles that a grapple-equipped log loader must clear, cycle after cycle, all shift long. The task breaks most grasping assumptions: hundreds of near-identical logs rest in frictional contact, the grapple captures several at once, every grasp reshapes the pile, and the machine only sees a partial, noisy surface.

My master’s thesis learns grasp targets, where to grasp and how to orient the grapple, directly from raw, unsegmented point clouds and carries the learned policy onto a physical forestry crane.

Eight environments clearing in parallel in simulation, then the same policy on the trailer-mounted hydraulic crane during a field trial. Full supplementary video.

Approach

  • Simulation. A GPU-parallel Isaac Lab environment of the crane, grapple and log pile, tuned from field measurements, supports demonstration collection and on-policy training across dozens of environments at once.
  • Policy. A PointNet-based network classifies at which observed point to grasp and predicts depth and grapple orientation there. The same outputs support behavior cloning from a privileged-state expert, reinforcement-learning fine-tuning (BC→RL) or training from scratch, and deployment.
  • Sim-to-real. A calibration campaign, carried out during a Mitacs Accelerate internship with FPInnovations, aligns simulated sensing and control with the physical testbed, and the policies run unchanged on the crane.

Results

In simulation, behavior cloning clears 98 of 100 piles of 200 logs, and BC→RL improves load stability. Twelve field trials compared a geometric heuristic baseline (which I also implemented), RL from scratch, BC, and BC→RL through complete grasp-transport-deposit cycles. BC and BC→RL deposited 93.8% and 88.9% of the pooled log inventory, against 80.4% for the heuristic, and BC→RL deposited logs on 83.6% of its cycles, against 79.6% for the heuristic and 65.7% for BC. The learned policies did this while observing unfiltered point clouds that still contained the storage rack’s rails and poles.

The paper is on arXiv and under review for ICRA 2027 (Sideris et al., 2026). The work was supported by an FRQNT master’s scholarship and a Mitacs Accelerate internship with FPInnovations, and is the subject of my M.Sc. thesis (Sideris, 2026) in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at McGill under Prof. Inna Sharf.

  2. Thesis
    Learning Autonomous Log Pile Clearing from Point Clouds
    George Sideris
    McGill University, 2026
    M.Sc. thesis, Department of Mechanical Engineering, submitted August 2026
    Abs Bib

    Automating log loading in mill yards requires deciding where to sink the grapple in a dense pile: hundreds of near-identical logs rest in frictional contact, the grapple captures bunches rather than single items, every grasp reshapes what remains, and the machine perceives the pile only through noisy depth sensing. This thesis learns that targeting decision directly from raw, unsegmented point clouds and carries the learned policy onto a physical forestry crane. A GPU-parallel simulation tuned from field measurements supports demonstration collection and on-policy training; a policy that scores every observed point and grasps at the highest-scoring one is trained by imitation of a privileged expert, then refined by reinforcement learning as a categorical distribution over those same points; and a calibration campaign aligns the simulated sensing and control with the physical testbed. Simulated evaluations and field trials on the crane compare the learned policies against the deployed heuristic baseline and demonstrate automated clearing of full log piles.
